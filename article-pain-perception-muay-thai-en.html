Pain Tolerance in Combat Sports. What Does the Physiology of Muay Thai Teach Us?

Published: 2024 | Read time: 5 min

The article is essentially based on a publication from 2024 in the journal Sport and Tourism. Muay Thai (Thai boxing) is notoriously famous for full-contact fighting involving devastating strikes utilizing elbows and knees. Together with Jakub Gwadera, we determined to scrutinize whether years of enduring extreme training regimens and absorbing savage strikes inherently altered how a fighter's biological organism perceives pain.

Methodology: How Do We Actually Measure Pain?

20 active fighters (boasting a minimum of exactly 2 years of hard training experience) willfully participated in the clinical study. We rigorously divided them into amateurs (those who combat whilst wearing protective gear) and hardcore professionals (those who battle bare without any padding). We utilized a pressure algometer (a highly sensitive medical tool designed to precisely record the absolute pain threshold in kilograms), pressing it relentlessly against areas most commonly pounded during an active fight: the thighs, shins, and forearms.

Study Results: Neural System Adaptation

  • Professionals exhibited a spectacularly higher overarching pain tolerance alongside an elevated pain threshold compared unequivocally to amateurs.
  • This profound biological phenomenon is formally designated as habituation. The nervous system of seasoned professionals, being relentlessly subjected to a barrage of microtraumas and constant exposure to agonizing stimuli (e.g., during the repetitive shin-blocking of heavy kicks), undergoes a literal "reprogramming"—it inherently ceases to process an incoming blow as a severe, critical bodily threat.

Conclusions for Physiotherapists and Coaches

These startling results possess colossal functional and practical significance. They starkly expose the biological divide separating amateur sports (shielded in armor) from the professional leagues. From the acute standpoint of clinical physiotherapy, we must vigilantly remember that an astronomically elevated pain threshold within professionals can simultaneously be hazardous—the organism might essentially fail to transmit crucial warning signals amidst serious underlying tissue degradation, decisively necessitating vastly more attentive and profound diagnostics from our end.

Full text of the scientific paper:
Gwadera J., Mosler D. (2024). Differences in pain perception between amateur and professional Muay Thai fighters. Sport i Turystyka. Środkowoeuropejskie Czasopismo Naukowe, 7(2), 103-120.
